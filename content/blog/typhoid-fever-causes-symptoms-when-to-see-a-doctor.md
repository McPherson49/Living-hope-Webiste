Typhoid fever is a serious bacterial infection that is common in places where safe water and sanitation are hard to guarantee — including parts of Nigeria. It is treatable, especially when caught early, but it can cause dangerous complications if it is ignored or treated wrongly.

## What causes typhoid?

Typhoid is caused by a bacterium called *Salmonella* Typhi. It spreads through food or water contaminated with the faeces of an infected person. This can happen when:

- drinking water is unsafe or has been contaminated by flooding or leaking pipes;
- food is prepared or served with unwashed hands;
- fruit, vegetables or ice are washed or made with unsafe water;
- food is left uncovered or reheated poorly.

## Symptoms to look out for

Symptoms usually appear one to three weeks after exposure and often build up gradually:

- a fever that rises steadily and lasts for days;
- headache and weakness;
- stomach pain;
- loss of appetite;
- constipation or diarrhoea;
- sometimes a faint rash on the chest or tummy.

Because malaria and typhoid can look alike — and can even occur together — a doctor will usually test for both rather than guess.

## Warning signs: seek help urgently

Typhoid can become dangerous, particularly in the second or third week without proper treatment. Go to an emergency unit straight away if there is:

- severe stomach pain or a swollen, hard tummy;
- blood in the stool or black stools;
- persistent vomiting;
- confusion, extreme weakness or drowsiness;
- a very high fever that does not settle.

## Why testing matters

A fever that lasts more than a day or two should always be checked. Typhoid is confirmed with laboratory tests. Blood culture is the more reliable test, while the widely used Widal test can be unreliable on its own, so a doctor will consider it alongside your symptoms and other results.

## How is typhoid treated?

Typhoid is treated with antibiotics prescribed by a doctor, along with rest and plenty of fluids. Please:

- take the full course exactly as prescribed, even if you feel better;
- never buy antibiotics without a prescription or take leftovers — this can hide the illness and helps bacteria become resistant to treatment;
- go back to your doctor if you are not improving after a few days.

Severe cases need admission to hospital for closer care.

## Preventing typhoid in your home

- Drink safe water: boiled and cooled, treated, or from a sealed, trusted source.
- Wash your hands with soap and water before eating or preparing food, and after using the toilet.
- Eat food that is freshly cooked and served hot; cover leftovers and reheat them thoroughly.
- Wash fruit and vegetables with safe water, and peel fruit where you can.
- Be careful with ice, and with food or drinks from sources you cannot trust.
- Keep toilets and food areas clean.

Typhoid vaccines are also available. Ask your doctor whether vaccination is recommended for your child or family — for example, before travel.
