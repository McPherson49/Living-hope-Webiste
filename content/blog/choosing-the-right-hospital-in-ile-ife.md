The best time to choose a hospital is before you need one. When someone in your family is suddenly unwell — or a baby decides to arrive at midnight — there is no time to compare options. A little thinking ahead means you will know exactly where to go, and who to call.

Here are the questions worth asking about any hospital in Ile-Ife, including ours.

## 1. How easy is it to get there?

Distance matters more than most people expect. In an emergency, minutes count, so think about the real journey: how long it takes from your home, workplace or campus, at different times of day, and whether the route is passable in the rainy season.

Living Hope Hospital is in Parakin, minutes from OAU and central Ile-Ife, which is one reason many families, students and staff in the area choose us. Whichever hospital you consider, try the route once when you are not in a hurry.

## 2. What can it do under one roof?

The more a hospital can do on site, the fewer referrals, repeat journeys and delays you face. Ask whether it has:

- emergency care and a way to reach it quickly;
- a laboratory for blood tests and screening;
- imaging such as X-ray or ultrasound;
- a pharmacy, so you can collect medicines after your consultation;
- maternity and children’s care, if you are planning a family.

You can see everything we offer on our [services page](/services).

## 3. Who will be caring for you?

Ask about the doctors’ qualifications, whether they are registered with the Medical and Dental Council of Nigeria (MDCN), and which specialists are available. A good hospital is happy to introduce its team. You can read about ours on the [doctors page](/doctors).

## 4. Is it ready for emergencies?

Ask plainly: Is there an emergency phone number? Is the emergency unit open around the clock? Is there an ambulance service? A good hospital gives straight answers. Save the emergency number in your phone now — not when you need it.

## 5. Are costs and insurance clear?

You should be able to find out how payment works and, for planned care, get an idea of costs before treatment begins. If you use an HMO, check that your plan is accepted. Our [insurance and HMO page](/insurance-hmo) explains how we handle this, and our billing team is happy to explain costs upfront.

## 6. Does it feel clean, calm and respectful?

Cleanliness, privacy and dignity matter, especially in maternity and children’s care. If you can, visit before you need care and notice how you are greeted, how tidy the wards and toilets are, and how staff speak to patients.

## 7. How well do they communicate?

Do they answer the phone? Do they explain things in plain language and welcome your questions? Simple booking by phone, WhatsApp or an online form is a good sign that a hospital respects your time.

## 8. What do other patients say?

Ask neighbours, colleagues and friends about their experiences, and read reviews with a balanced eye. A few complaints about waiting time are normal; patterns of poor communication or unclean facilities are not.

## A quick checklist

- Do I know how to reach the hospital quickly, day and night?
- Do I know its emergency number, and is it saved in my phone?
- Are the services my family is likely to need available there?
- Do I know the doctors’ qualifications and registration?
- Do I understand how payment and insurance work?
- Did the place feel clean, calm and respectful?
