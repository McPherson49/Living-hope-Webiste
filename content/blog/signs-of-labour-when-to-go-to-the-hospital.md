In the last weeks of pregnancy, almost every mother asks the same question: *How will I know it’s time?* Knowing the signs — and having a plan — helps you feel calmer, and helps you and your baby get the care you need at the right moment.

## Signs that labour may be near

These can appear days or even weeks before labour begins:

- **Your baby “drops”** lower into your pelvis, and you may breathe more easily but feel more pressure below.
- **Practice contractions** (often called Braxton Hicks) — irregular tightenings that usually ease when you rest, drink water or change position.
- **A “show”** — a small amount of thick, pinkish or blood-tinged mucus.
- **Backache, cramps or loose stools.**

## Signs that labour has started

True labour usually brings:

- **Contractions that come regularly** and get stronger, longer and closer together, and do not ease when you rest or move.
- **Pain that starts in your back** and moves around to the front, or a tight, cramping pain low in your tummy.
- **Your waters breaking** — either a gush or a steady trickle of fluid.

## When should I leave for the hospital?

Many hospitals use a simple guide: go when contractions come about every **5 minutes**, last about **1 minute**, and have been like that for about **1 hour**. But your own doctor may give you different advice, especially if:

- this is not your first baby, because labour can be faster;
- you have had a caesarean section before;
- your pregnancy is high risk, or you are expecting twins;
- you live far from the hospital, or travel is difficult at night or in the rains.

In these cases, it is safer to leave earlier. If you are unsure, call the hospital and describe what is happening — nobody minds a “false alarm”.

## Go straight away if…

Do not wait for contractions to become regular if:

- your waters break — especially if the fluid is green, brown or smells unpleasant;
- you have vaginal bleeding heavier than a light spotting;
- your baby is moving much less than usual;
- you have constant, severe pain with no break between contractions;
- you have a severe headache, blurred vision, or sudden swelling of your face or hands;
- you have a fever;
- you notice signs of labour before 37 weeks of pregnancy.

## Get ready before the day comes

- **Pack your hospital bag** by around 36 weeks, with your ID, antenatal card and records, clothes for you and your baby, toiletries and phone charger.
- **Plan your transport**, and a back-up — including for the middle of the night.
- **Save important numbers** in your phone: the hospital, your doctor and your birth companion.
- **Know the route** and how long it takes.
- **Agree who will look after** any other children.
- **Ask questions** at your antenatal visits — about pain relief, birth choices and when to come in.
