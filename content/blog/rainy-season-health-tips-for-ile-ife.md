In Ile-Ife, the rains usually arrive around April and continue into October. They bring welcome relief from the heat — and a few health risks that families do well to prepare for. Here is what to watch for, and what to do about it.

## Mosquitoes and malaria

Standing water is where mosquitoes breed, and there is plenty of it in the rainy season. To reduce the risk:

- clear blocked gutters and drains, and empty water from old tyres, buckets and tins;
- cover water storage containers;
- sleep under an insecticide-treated net every night;
- screen windows and doors where you can.

If someone develops a fever, get tested rather than guessing. Read more in our guide to [malaria symptoms and treatment](/blog/malaria-symptoms-and-treatment).

## Unsafe water and food

Heavy rain and flooding can contaminate wells, tanks and streets. That raises the risk of typhoid, diarrhoea and other stomach infections. To stay safe:

- drink water that has been boiled and cooled, treated, or comes from a sealed, trusted source;
- wash your hands with soap before eating or preparing food, and after using the toilet;
- keep food covered, and eat it freshly cooked and hot;
- wash fruit and vegetables with safe water.

Our guide to [typhoid fever](/blog/typhoid-fever-causes-symptoms-when-to-see-a-doctor) explains the symptoms to watch for.

## Coughs, colds and chest infections

Damp weather and sudden changes in temperature make colds and chest infections more common, especially in children and older adults. Keep children dry and warm, wash hands often, and make sure people with a cough cover their mouth. See a doctor promptly if there is difficulty breathing, fast breathing in a child, or a fever that will not settle.

## Skin and fungal infections

Wet clothes and shoes are a perfect home for fungal infections. Change out of wet clothing promptly, dry your skin — especially between the toes and in skin folds — and do not share towels. Clean cuts and scrapes straight away, especially after wading through floodwater, and see a doctor if a wound becomes red, swollen, painful or starts to discharge.

## Flooded roads and storms

- Avoid walking or driving through floodwater. It can hide open drains and sharp objects, and it is often contaminated.
- During thunderstorms, stay indoors, and do not shelter under isolated trees.
- Keep electrical appliances and sockets away from water.
- Watch children closely near drains, gutters and fast-flowing water.

## Quick rainy-season checklist for your home

- Nets checked and hung.
- Drains and gutters cleared.
- Safe drinking water ready.
- Food stored and covered.
- Dry clothes and shoes to change into.
- Emergency number saved in your phone.

## When to see a doctor

Don’t wait it out if:

- a fever lasts more than a day or two — or straight away in a baby, a small child or a pregnant woman;
- there is diarrhoea with signs of dehydration, such as a very dry mouth, very little urine, sunken eyes or unusual sleepiness;
- someone has difficulty breathing;
- a wound is getting worse.
